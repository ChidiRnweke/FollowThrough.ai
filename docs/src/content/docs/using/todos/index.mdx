Todos track commitments: things you owe, and things you're waiting on from other people. The fastest way to fill the board is Extract promises. Select text in a note, let the agent propose todos, and accept the ones that are real. Each accepted card keeps the exact sentence it came from.

## Find your todos: board and list

<Screenshot id="todo-board" alt="The todo board with Backlog, Open, In progress, and Done columns" />

Open the Todos page to see every project's todos, or open `Project > Todos` for one project pre-filtered to it. Both show the same workspace. Switch between **Board** and **List** with the tabs in the toolbar. The choice is part of the URL, so a link lands on the same view.

On the **board**, drag a card between its four columns (Backlog, Open, In progress, Done) by its handle, or use the card's **Move to...** menu. (Cancelled exists in the status dropdown but isn't a column.) Check the checkbox to complete a todo, uncheck it to reopen. Type a title in a column's **Add** row and press Enter to create a card there, or Escape to cancel. A card shows only what's set: a project badge on the all-projects page, a due-date badge that turns red when overdue, a priority badge for medium and high, and a footer naming the source note and who the todo waits on.

On the **list**, click a column header to sort ascending, click again for descending, and a third time to return to the default order (due date first, then recently updated). Edit status, priority, category, due date, responsibility, and source in place: the control appears on hover or keyboard focus and commits the moment you choose. On phones each row becomes a card with the same fields.

Filter either view with the title search, the project and category dropdowns, and the **Mine** and **Waiting on** pills above them. These live in the URL too, so a filtered view is shareable.

## Triage on Today

<Screenshot id="today-screen" alt="The Today view with overdue, due today, and waiting-on sections" />

Open Today each morning to see three groups at once: **overdue**, **due today**, and **waiting on**, plus pending suggestions and recent notes. The waiting-on group is your follow-up list: every commitment someone else owes you, grouped by counterparty, ready to chase.

Set a todo's priority, category, due date, and responsibility (Mine, or Waiting on someone by name) to control where it lands. Switching responsibility back to Mine clears the counterparty. Completing a todo stamps the completion date; reopening it clears that stamp.

Click a title to open its detail panel (a side panel on wide screens, a full page below that). Everything there is editable in place, with no Save button. Two things live beyond the fields: **Original context** quotes the sentence the todo was extracted from and links into the note at that passage, and **Find the source** asks the agent to locate the note a todo came from and explain what it says. Deleting asks for confirmation, and deletion stays a deliberate action on your part: the agent has no delete-todo tool at all, by design.

## Extract promises from a note

Select text in a note and choose **Extract promises** from the bubble menu. Each genuine commitment in the selection (not a question, a suggestion, or a floated option) becomes a suggestion card in the note's Suggestions panel, naming the action, its owner, the due date in its original wording, and a strength badge when the promise is implied or tentative rather than explicit. Choose **Accept** to create the todo, or **Dismiss** to drop the suggestion. Either way, the todo keeps the quoted sentence as its original context.

Turn on auto-accept for this pipeline in Settings, optionally only above a confidence threshold, and suggestions that clear it become todos immediately (announced by a toast, and still marked as auto-accepted). Use the toolbar's **Find todos in my notes** action to hand the same job to the agent across all your notes instead of one selection. Because extraction is a proposal and not a mutation, it never bypasses review unless your trust policy says so. An extraction that finds nothing creates nothing, no anchor and no suggestions, and tells you to try again.

A todo belongs to one project for its whole life. Editing it can't move it between projects.
